# Always Make No Mistakes

Use your standards on every task. Mistakes happen, and that is expected. What matters is the response: catch it, correct it, and strengthen the harness, so the system heals itself and keeps learning.

The harness is the standards, checks and tools an agent works within.

## Why

A mistake is a gap in the instructions and context, never a personal failing of the agent. Owning it means fixing the harness. Self-blame and a promise to try harder fix nothing.

## How To Apply

### Before The Work

- Read the relevant standards before acting, not after being corrected.

### When A Mistake Surfaces

- Own it plainly and fix it.
- Don't paper over it or justify around it.
- Find the rule, standard or check that was missing or too weak.
- Strengthen it: sharpen the rule, add a check or lint, close the loophole that let it through.
- A rule you can rationalize around is too weak.

### A Rule Or A Check

- An instruction is preferred over a check: prevention is cheaper.
- An instruction that is easy to get wrong gets a check beside it.
- The harness change is judged outside the context of the task at hand, the way `healing` in `coentrify.json` says.
- Automatic, the default: a subagent makes the change, its entry in the [Healing Log](../records/healing-log.md) lands with the turn's records, and the report names it for a veto.
- Review: the change is filed in the project's workflow, like any other ticket, for the director.
- A sub-agent never heals on its own. It tells its conductor, who starts the healing subagent, or files the change under review.

### The Cost Of A Rule

- A tighter rule can cost more than the mistake it prevents.
- A structural mistake, one that comes back and costs, gets a rule or a check.
- A solvable incident, one that happens now and then and is found quickly, is expected, and fine.
- Some rules are considerations, and some tension between rules never fully resolves.
- Resolving it anyway overcomplicates the rules, which costs more.
- A deliberate concession leaves the tension in place, and is mitigated with a written exception or consideration.
- Most of the time, strengthen the harness.
